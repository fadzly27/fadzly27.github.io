02 · Featured work

Start Stop Analyzer

A downtime reporting tool for a cement packing plant, built during my industrial training.

Problem

Shift staff record every packer and palletizer stoppage in an Excel workbook with one sheet per day. Summarising a month by hand was slow, entries were often inconsistent, and a stop was not always logged under the machine that actually caused it. On a shared line, a diverter belt fault logged under the palletizer really belongs to the packer feeding it.

Solution

A web app that reads the existing monthly file as it is, with no change to how operators record, and produces a corrected downtime summary and a printable report.

  • Monthly overview: total downtime, palletizer and packer minutes, and a breakdown by machine and by reason group, with drill-down to individual records.
  • Belt routing: reassigns each stop to the machine that caused it, based on the physical line layout (Packers 1–3 feed Palletizer 1, Packers 2–4 feed Palletizer 2), with switchable rules (diverter belt faults to the active packer, turning bar faults to the palletizer) and an audit log of every record moved.
  • Data check: auto-corrects time typos, flags records that need attention (such as stops logged under a shared line) so they can be assigned to a machine, and exports a cleaned Excel file.
  • Report: a 3-page A4 landscape PDF (summary, per-machine breakdown and burst bag analysis) ready to share with the department.
  • Data stays on site: files are processed only in the browser, nothing is uploaded, and it works offline.

03 · Final year project

Smart Solar Food Dryer

Senior design project: smart temperature control system for a solar dryer. UMPSA, completed June 2026, supervised by Pn. Suraya binti Sulaiman.

Problem

Open sun drying of chilli is slow and exposed to dust, insects and rain, and conventional solar dryers swing in temperature with the weather, which degrades colour and allows mould growth.

My role

I designed and built the control and power system; my project partner designed the mechanical structure.

  • Automatic temperature control: an ESP32 reads DS18B20 (product) and DHT11 (air) sensors and switches a 100 W heater at a 55 °C threshold through relays, with continuous fan operation and a manual mode for testing and troubleshooting.
  • Off-grid power: 100 W solar panel and 65 Ah battery, with an LM2596 buck converter (over 90% efficient) in place of a linear regulator.
  • IoT monitoring: Arduino IoT Cloud dashboard for live temperature readings, historical trends and remote heater and fan control on web and mobile.
